Abstract

  1. Question. Can machine learning models improve on FlyRank's existing hand-written flag system for prioritizing content pages vulnerable to future organic search traffic decay, to guide scarce human editorial review bandwidth?
  2. Data & Method. Across 79M+ rows of FlyRank enterprise search-performance data, we apply activity-based eligibility criteria to analyze 16,513 eligible pages across 36 client domains. We train a standardized Logistic Regression classifier on 9 pre-May historical features under a client-grouped holdout design (GroupShuffleSplit, test_size=0.20, 8 held-out clients) to predict whether May organic search clicks decline by ≥20% relative to April.
  3. Primary Result. On the primary client-held-out test partition (Seed 42, N = 1,727 pages), Logistic Regression achieved a Precision@50 of 0.2400 (12/50) compared to 0.2000 (10/50) for a transparent 3-signal heuristic baseline, a single-split lift of +0.0400 (+4.0 pp).
  4. Validation Caveat. An expanded 5-split stability audit showed that observed lift varies substantially across client partitions (mean lift: +1.6 pp, range: −12.0 to +14.0 pp), with Logistic Regression outperforming the baseline in 3 of 5 splits.
  5. Practical Use. Predictions are translated into a decision-support Content Action Playbook with 5 review archetypes, explainable reason codes, illustrative planning effort assumptions, and human-in-the-loop safety guardrails that prohibit autonomous CMS modifications. An exploratory investigation (§06–§08) separately found that a richer trajectory feature representation produced a 74% Precision@50 single-split result (Seed 42), though repeated client-grouped validation showed 54.8% ± 10.6 percentage points across five seeds.

§01
Lane: Refresh / Content Opportunity Scoring

Problem Framing

Enterprise digital publishers manage content libraries spanning thousands of URLs. Organic search traffic can change over time, but editorial teams operate under finite diagnostic bandwidth and cannot manually investigate every page with equal depth.

This gap already exists inside FlyRank's own product. FlyRank's platform ships hand-written flags today — a health score, quick-win tags, needs-attention markers — each built from thresholds someone chose by hand. They work, and they run in production. But fixed rules run out once signals multiply and start moving together across a large client portfolio, and no trained model has replaced them yet. This capstone tests whether a learned model can close that specific gap for one decision: which pages to route into editorial review first.

This research supports candidate queue prioritization for human editorial content review. Rather than attempting to automate editorial interventions or reverse-engineer search engine ranking algorithms, the model ranks URLs by their predicted likelihood of meeting a subsequent click-decay label, allowing editorial teams to direct diagnostic attention toward pages showing empirical decline signals.

Unit of Analysis & Target Decision

  • Unit of analysis: An individual published URL (content_hash_id) within a client domain (client_hash_id) over the May 1–31, 2026 forward evaluation window.
  • Output: A deterministic, ranked decision-support candidate queue containing predicted decay probabilities, 5 review archetype tags, explainable reason codes, illustrative planning effort, and safety guardrails.
  • Human action: Content editors conduct structured diagnostic audits based on the assigned archetype.
  • Primary evaluation queue: Top 50 candidates.

Cost of Errors

Error type Consequence
False positive Allocates illustrative editorial review effort (~2–5 hours per page) to a page that does not meet the May decline label.
False negative A page that subsequently meets the decline label receives lower review priority.

Because human review bandwidth is strictly capped, ranking precision at the top of the queue (Precision@50) serves as the primary evaluation metric rather than overall portfolio recall.

Decision chain Model Signal → Review Priority → Human Diagnosis → Editorial Decision

§03

Baseline & Method

Week 4 Heuristic Baseline

A 3-signal heuristic rule establishes a transparent, non-learned benchmark. For each page, the baseline computes the mean daily score across its available April records:

Daily Score_d = 1{GSC impressions ≥ 10} + 1{avg position > 10} + 1{GA4 pageviews ≥ 1} Baseline Score = mean(Daily Score) across available April records

Tie-breaking cascade

The baseline and the model use different tiebreaks, both deterministic:

Baseline: Score DESC → April Impressions DESC → April Pageviews DESC → Avg Position ASC Model: Score DESC → Content ID ASC

Supervised Classification Pipeline

A standardized Logistic Regression pipeline with balanced class weighting (w05_model.ipynb): 

Pipeline([
    ("scaler", StandardScaler()),
    ("model", LogisticRegression(
        class_weight="balanced",
        random_state=42,
        max_iter=1000
    ))
])

Target Label

A binary decay label indicating whether May organic search clicks dropped by ≥20% relative to April:

y = 1{ clicks_May < 0.80 x clicks_Apr }
  • Portfolio base rate: 41.62% (6,873 declining pages out of 16,513)
  • Held-out test base rate: 38.68% (668 declining pages out of 1,727)

The 9 Official Modeling Features

5 continuous historical metrics (4 log-transformed) + 4 boolean platform availability flags. All bounded to the Feb–Apr window.

  1. 1log_gsc_impressions_feb_aprlog(1 + ImpressionsFeb–Apr) — search exposure volume.
  2. 2log_gsc_clicks_aprlog(1 + ClicksApr) — baseline traffic volume.
  3. 3gsc_avg_position_aprAverage Search Console ranking position during April 2026.
  4. 4log_ga4_pageviews_feb_aprlog(1 + PageviewsFeb–Apr) — historical on-site consumption.
  5. 5log_ga4_engaged_sessions_feb_aprlog(1 + Engaged SessionsFeb–Apr) — historical engagement.
  6. 6client_has_gscBoolean — Search Console access configured pre-May.
  7. 7client_has_ga4Boolean — GA4 analytics access configured pre-May.
  8. 8gsc_data_availableBoolean — active Search Console tracking confirmed.
  9. 9ga4_data_availableBoolean — active GA4 tracking confirmed.

Metric Granularity & Base Rate Context

Precision@50 measures the concentration of true declining pages in the top 50 prioritized candidates. At K = 50, each correctly prioritized page changes Precision@50 by exactly 2 percentage points (1/50 = 0.02), so small differences should be interpreted cautiously.

The held-out test base rate on Seed 42 is 38.68% (668 declining pages out of 1,727). The model's Precision@50 of 24.0% (12/50) reflects candidate queue triage rather than unranked accuracy; historical search signals alone do not concentrate declining pages into the top rank without substantial false positives (38 FP / 50).

Operational reality At a 24% precision rate, 38 of the top 50 candidates did not meet the May decline label. The model functions strictly as candidate triage — a prompt for human diagnostic review, never an automated action trigger.

§10

Model Interpretation

The standardized Logistic Regression coefficients describe directional associations within the fitted model. They are not causal effects.

Feature Std. coefficient Interpretation
log_ga4_pageviews_feb_apr −0.9394 Higher historical on-site pageviews have a negative coefficient in the fitted model.
ga4_data_available +0.7060 Active analytics instrumentation presence indicator.
gsc_avg_position_apr +0.3212 Higher average position (worse ranking) has a positive coefficient.
log_gsc_clicks_apr +0.2807 Higher baseline clicks have a positive coefficient. Because the target is a percentage decline from April, this is a model association — not evidence that higher volume causes decay.
client_has_ga4 −0.1751 Platform configuration indicator, negative fitted coefficient.
log_ga4_engaged_sessions_feb_apr +0.0446 Engagement-volume feature with a small positive fitted coefficient.
log_gsc_impressions_feb_apr −0.0231 Search-exposure feature with a small negative fitted coefficient.
gsc_data_available 0.0000 Universal availability across active cohort — zero variance, no discriminative information.
client_has_gsc 0.0000 Universal configuration across active cohort — zero variance.

Key Surprises

  1. Positive coefficient on log_gsc_clicks_apr. Because the label is a percentage decline from April, pages with higher April clicks are more likely to meet the 20% threshold. This is a mathematical association within the dataset, not evidence of causation.
  2. Constant features. client_has_gsc and gsc_data_available showed zero variance across the eligible cohort and contributed no discriminative signal.
  3. Partition sensitivity. The +4.0 pp single-split lift is not stable across client partitions (range: −12.0 to +14.0 pp across 5 seeds).

The No-Go List — strictly prohibited from automation

§12

Limitations

  • Association is not causation. The study measures statistical association between pre-May historical features and the May decline label. It does not establish that executing a specific content edit will causally reverse decay.
  • Partition sensitivity. Primary-split improvement (+4.0 pp) is not stable across repeated partitions — lift ranged from −12.0 pp to +14.0 pp across 5 client-grouped seeds.
  • Top-of-queue metric only. Precision@50 evaluates the top 50 ranked candidates; it does not measure recall across the full portfolio.
  • In-sample queue disclosure. Full-portfolio queue scores include in-sample scores for pages from the original W05 training fit. The full queue is a decision-support prioritization artifact, not an out-of-sample estimate.
  • Illustrative effort figures. Editorial effort numbers (~2–5 h per page) are planning assumptions, not measured business costs.
  • No qualitative evaluation. The model uses aggregate numerical signals. It cannot evaluate brand voice, factual accuracy, regulatory compliance, or conversion-funnel fit.
  • No autonomous action. A model score alone does not diagnose why a page is vulnerable or establish which editorial change would improve performance. Every recommendation is a prompt for human diagnosis.
Final engineering conclusion: the Logistic Regression model provides a directional candidate-ranking signal that modestly exceeded the transparent baseline on the primary split, but its observed advantage is not stable enough to justify autonomous content decisions.
